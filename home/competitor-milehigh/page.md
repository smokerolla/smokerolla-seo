### The Domain Rating inversion

They have a **stronger link profile than you** (Domain Rating 32 vs 27, and a materially better Ahrefs Rank) while earning **a quarter of your organic traffic**. That is the most informative number in this audit.

It means their links are not doing any work. Authority sits on their homepage and never reaches the pages that need to rank — the same disease your own audit diagnosed on your collections, in a more advanced stage. It also means your 1,679 visits are earned through content and relevance rather than authority, which is the more durable of the two positions.

Practical consequence: **do not read their Domain Rating as a reason you can't outrank them.** On the keywords in Finding 5 they rank at position 20+ *with* DR 32. Link strength is not what decides these SERPs.

## Finding 1 — One page carries 66% of their non-branded traffic, and it is brand new

[/collections/dab-pens](https://www.milehighglasspipes.com/collections/dab-pens) pulls **265 visits/month** from a single keyword: **"dab pen for wax"** — 4,400 searches/month, **difficulty 0**, they sit at position 8.

Ahrefs marks this page `newly_ranked` in the last 3 months. It went from nothing to 265 visits. That one page is the entire reason their traffic grew at all this quarter.

This keyword is already **#2 on your own priority shortlist** (traffic potential 28k, difficulty 0). A competitor with zero referring domains on that page just proved it is winnable with nothing but a collection page that exists. You have the catalogue depth they don't.

## Finding 2 — Their headline number is decaying underneath

Comparing against 3 months ago, across their top 30 pages:

- Total: 466 → 502 visits. Looks like growth.
- But their **homepage lost 270 visits** (371 → 101) — a 73% collapse in branded search.
- And `/collections/bongs-water-pipes-under-30` fell 32 → 5.

Strip out the one new dab-pens page and **they are down roughly 230 visits**. The business is shrinking; a single lucky category page masks it. They are not a rising threat — they are a declining shop with one good page.

## Finding 3 — A quarter of their traffic is their own name

98 of 511 keyword-visits are branded: "mile high glass", "mile high smoke shop", "mile high pipe and tobacco". That is Denver local-shop demand, not national e-commerce demand — note the `local_pack` SERP feature on those queries.

Non-branded, they are a **~300 visit/month site**. Their commercial footprint is smaller than the headline suggests, and it is concentrated in exactly four pages.

## Finding 4 — They rank on nothing but Google's tolerance

Median referring domains across their top 40 pages: **zero**. Only the homepage has a real link profile (415 referring domains, URL Rating 7). Every category page ranking for real volume — dab-pens, lookah, glow-in-the-dark — has **0 referring domains and URL Rating 4 or less**.

This is the same structural weakness your audit found on your own collections. The read-across matters: in this niche, category pages rank without links. That means **internal linking and on-page merchandising are sufficient to win** — you do not need a link-building budget to take these keywords.

## Finding 6 — They resell your brand, and outrank you on it

They operate **[/collections/smokerolla](https://www.milehighglasspipes.com/collections/smokerolla)** — ranking position 6 for "klean bong" (70/mo), 5 visits/month.

This is a commercial question, not an SEO one. A wholesale customer is running a branded collection page for Smokerolla and capturing branded search that should land on your own store. Worth checking against your B2B account list, and worth deciding whether your wholesale terms should say anything about branded-search bidding or branded collection pages.

Two structural notes from this table:

- **Your two closest keyword overlaps are content sites, not shops.** [tvape.com](https://tvape.com) (91 shared keywords, review blog) and [kingpalm.com](https://kingpalm.com) (91 shared, a brand site) both beat you on the same terms with *editorial* pages. That matches your own audit finding — 77% of your organic traffic is already blog-driven. The competitors winning your keywords are winning them with content, which is the asset class you already have.
- **Mile High's competitor set skews wholesale** ([mjwholesale.com](https://mjwholesale.com), [spswholesale.com](https://spswholesale.com)) while yours skews review/brand ([tvape.com](https://tvape.com), [vaporizerwizard.com](https://www.vaporizerwizard.com), [420vapezone.com](https://420vapezone.com)). You are competing in a different, more consumer-facing part of the market than they are — another reason to treat them as a data point rather than a rival.

### What this reframes

Mile High is worth reading for **evidence** (zero-difficulty category keywords are winnable without links) but not for **threat assessment**. If you want a competitor to benchmark against, the right target is Smoke Cartel or DankGeek — and the right question is which of their 7,000-10,000 keywords overlap your existing catalogue. That is a separate analysis; say the word.

## What to do

1. **Take "dab pen for wax" (4,400/mo, KD 0).** You already have the products. This is the highest-value single action on this page, and it is on your existing shortlist — this audit is the proof it works.
2. **Claim the seven unclaimed keywords above.** All difficulty 0, all held by a declining competitor at position 12-27. Most map to collections you already have; this is the same tag-and-internal-link work already underway.
3. **Do not treat them as a threat.** Non-branded ~300 visits and falling. No link profile. The competitive read is: this niche has no authority moat, so distribution work wins.
4. **Check whether Mile High is a wholesale account of yours** and decide the branded-collection policy.
5. **Benchmark against Smoke Cartel / DankGeek instead**, not Mile High — they are the tier that actually sets the ceiling on these SERPs.
